# Ahn, Jeong, Kim, Yun (2025) — *Holographic Reconstruction of Black Hole Spacetime: Machine Learning and Entanglement Entropy*

## One-sentence takeaway
This is the closest methodological template for your ML goal: it reconstructs static isotropic AdS black-hole metrics from boundary entanglement entropy data using neural ODEs and Monte-Carlo integration.

## What problem the paper addresses
The paper treats bulk metric reconstruction as an inverse holographic problem. Standard holographic workflows start with a bulk metric and compute entanglement entropy using the Ryu-Takayanagi formula. This paper reverses that direction: given entanglement entropy data on the boundary, infer the bulk metric functions.

The authors emphasize that earlier deep-learning approaches often reconstructed discrete metrics or assumed a simplified metric ansatz with only one unknown function. Their goal is to reconstruct a continuous and more general isotropic metric with two unknown radial functions.

## Setup and method
The bulk ansatz is a static asymptotically AdS4 metric of the form

\[
ds^2 = \frac{L^2}{z^2}\left[-f(z)dt^2 + \frac{dz^2}{f(z)} + h(z)(dx^2+dy^2)\right].
\]

For a strip-shaped boundary region, the Ryu-Takayanagi formula gives two coupled integral relations: one for the strip width \(\ell(z_*)\) and one for the finite part of the entanglement entropy \(S_{\mathrm{finite}}(z_*)\). The unknown functions \(f(z)\) and \(h(z)\) enter these integrals.

The authors use a neural ODE/neural integration framework to represent continuous metric functions. Monte-Carlo integration is used inside the training loop to evaluate the RT integrals. The loss compares the predicted \(S_{\mathrm{finite}}(\ell)\) relation to the input entanglement data.

They choose a metric parametrization that imposes the horizon condition and asymptotic AdS behavior by construction. In particular, \(f(1)=0\) and \(f(0)=h(0)=1\) are built into the ansatz, reducing the burden on optimization.

## Main results
The paper first reviews Bilson’s reconstruction method, which can invert entanglement data to reconstruct \(f(z)\) when \(h(z)=1\). It then explains why this is insufficient for generic metrics: a single entanglement entropy function does not algebraically determine both \(f(z)\) and \(h(z)\) by Bilson’s analytic method alone.

The neural ODE method successfully reconstructs known holographic examples, including the linear-axion model, the Gubser-Rocha model, and holographic superconductors. The method is also applied to entanglement entropy data from a one-dimensional fermionic tight-binding chain at half filling, yielding an emergent bulk metric. The authors note a similarity between the reconstructed tight-binding metric and the Gubser-Rocha metric and speculate that it may be tied to metallic behavior.

## Important ideas for your project
- Treat the geometry as trainable continuous functions, not as a finite set of disconnected radial grid values.
- Build asymptotic AdS and horizon constraints into the neural ansatz where possible.
- Put the holographic integral map inside the training loop: the network predicts metric functions, the integrator predicts entanglement entropy, and the loss compares to boundary data.
- Use exactly solvable or analytically controlled cases, such as pure AdS and BTZ, as calibration targets before moving to Vaidya-AdS.

## Relevance to reconstructing Vaidya-AdS from boundary entanglement data
This paper is static, while your final target is time-dependent Vaidya-AdS. The conceptual extension is clear but nontrivial: replace the RT minimal-surface integral map by an HRT geodesic/equation-of-motion map in the Vaidya geometry. Instead of reconstructing radial functions \(f(z),h(z)\), you would likely reconstruct a time-dependent mass function \(m(v)\), or a more general dynamical metric ansatz.

A practical roadmap inspired by this paper is:

1. reproduce BTZ reconstruction from synthetic entanglement data;
2. use pure AdS and BTZ as endpoints for Vaidya data validation;
3. train on synthetic Vaidya data \(S(l,t)\) generated by HRT geodesics;
4. parameterize \(m(v)\) with a small neural network or spline and train it through the geodesic solver;
5. later generalize from reconstructing \(m(v)\) to reconstructing less constrained dynamical geometries.
