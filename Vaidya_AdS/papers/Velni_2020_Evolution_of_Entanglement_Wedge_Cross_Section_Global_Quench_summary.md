# Babaei Velni, Mohammadi Mozaffar, Vahidinia (2020) — *Evolution of Entanglement Wedge Cross Section Following a Global Quench*

## One-sentence takeaway
This paper extends Vaidya-quench studies from ordinary entanglement entropy to the entanglement wedge cross section, a mixed-state correlation measure with characteristic early quadratic growth, intermediate linear growth, and late saturation after a thermal quench.

## What problem the paper addresses
Entanglement entropy is best suited to correlations between a subsystem and its complement, especially for pure states. For two disjoint or adjacent subregions in a mixed state, other correlation measures are needed. The entanglement wedge cross section (EWCS) is a bulk geometric quantity proposed to be dual to several mixed-state information measures, including entanglement of purification, reflected entropy, odd entropy, and logarithmic negativity.

The paper studies how EWCS evolves after a global quench modeled holographically by Vaidya collapse.

## Setup and method
The authors consider Vaidya geometries describing a thin shell of null matter collapsing into AdS to form a black brane. They study strip-shaped boundary subregions and compute the corresponding HRT surfaces and entanglement wedge cross sections. The analysis is carried out in both 2+1 and 3+1 bulk dimensions, with thermal quenches and electromagnetic quenches.

For the static reference cases, they review EWCS in pure AdS, AdS-Schwarzschild black branes, and extremal charged black branes. The time-dependent calculation then tracks how the EWCS interpolates between the vacuum and final equilibrium values.

## Main results
For a 2+1-dimensional bulk thermal quench ending in BTZ, the EWCS evolution has three regimes for large regions:

1. early-time quadratic growth;
2. intermediate linear growth;
3. late-time saturation.

The same qualitative structure appears for higher-dimensional thermal quenches. The linear regime becomes more pronounced for larger entangling regions. The growth rate in the linear regime is related to the entanglement velocity known from holographic entanglement entropy studies.

For electromagnetic quenches, turning on charge slows thermalization. In the zero-temperature extremal limit, the intermediate linear growth is replaced by logarithmic growth, tied to the double zero of the blackening factor at the extremal horizon.

## Important ideas for your project
- EWCS provides information beyond single-interval entanglement entropy.
- It is sensitive to the connected/disconnected phase structure of the entanglement wedge.
- Mixed-state correlation observables may help constrain bulk reconstruction beyond what single-interval entropy can provide.
- Vaidya data can be generalized from \(S(l,t)\) to multi-region observables.

## Relevance to reconstructing Vaidya-AdS from boundary entanglement data
This paper is probably not the first step for your reconstruction project, because EWCS requires a more complicated multi-surface calculation than single-interval HRT geodesic length. However, it is a strong future extension. If reconstructing the Vaidya mass function from ordinary entanglement data is underconstrained or degenerate, adding mixed-state observables such as EWCS, mutual information, or reflected-entropy proxies could provide additional independent constraints on the bulk geometry.
